# Ticket — consumer app (wildcard)

**Assumption broken.** The other three directions all assume a food marketplace must *merchandise* — cover imagery, colour, appetite appeal. Ticket removes imagery entirely and sells with typography and numbers: every bag is a numbered chit on newsprint, the price is set in 52px mono, and the whole app looks like something printed rather than something rendered.

**Why Kuwait might reward it.** The audience is deal-fluent and already suspicious that pretty photos hide a worse bag — a chit that shows *what you pay, what it's worth, when it closes, who attested* reads as honesty, not austerity. It is legible at midday in a car with the sun on the screen, it loads instantly on a weak connection at a drive-up lane, and the redemption screen is a screenshot people actually want to post: the number is the flex.

**How it differs.** Monochrome ink on paper with one spot red, zero radii, dashed rules, hard 3px offset instead of shadow, Archivo + Alexandria with JetBrains Mono for every figure, 80ms motion (things appear, they don't animate).

**Risk.** It can read as unfinished to a first-time user. Mitigation: the inverted black header and the printed-chit redemption screen carry enough craft to signal that the plainness is deliberate.
